Every semester, our second session is "Prompting Well," and every semester it's the one people ask about most. Not because prompting is hard, exactly, but because the failure modes are easy to miss until you've watched a few dozen of them happen live.

## What we cover

We start with a live demo: the same rough task, asked three different ways, in front of the room. The instructions-context-examples framing does most of the work here — most bad prompts are missing one of the three, not all of them.

```txt
Bad:  "write me something about our club"
Good: "Write a 3-sentence Instagram caption for the AI Club's
       first meeting. Audience: first-years with no CS background.
       Tone: welcoming, a little funny. No emoji."
```

## Why we spend time on failure

We intentionally show broken outputs before polished ones. Watching a model confidently misunderstand an ambiguous ask is more instructive than watching it succeed — it's the fastest way to build the instinct for what a model needs to hear.

## Bring your own laptop

Bring a laptop and an account with any major model — we don't require a specific one. If you don't have access to anything, we have a few club logins to share for the night.
