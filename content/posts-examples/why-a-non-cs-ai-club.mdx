Most of the AI groups we looked at before starting this club assumed you already knew how to code. That's a reasonable assumption for a CS department event. It's the wrong assumption for a club that wants government majors, biologists, and studio art students in the room.

## Two tracks, one room

Every session in our [curriculum](/curriculum) is written for two tracks at once: a beginner track that assumes nothing beyond curiosity, and a track for people who've written code before and want to move faster. Same topic, same room, different pace.

## Why this matters right now

AI tools are showing up in every major at Bowdoin, not just computer science. Economics students are using them to explore datasets, English majors are debating what they mean for authorship, and pre-med students are asking how they'll change diagnosis. A club that only speaks to CS majors misses almost everyone actually affected by this shift.

## Come as you are

If you've never written a line of code, the [first few sessions](/curriculum) are built for you. If you've already fine-tuned a model on your laptop, we'd love your help teaching the sessions that come after that.
